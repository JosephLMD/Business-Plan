OneWhat this document is, and why it exists

MicroAire is not being asked to believe that Colombia is a good market. The procedure counts are published by ISAPS and republished by the Colombian Society of Plastic Surgery, and anyone can check them.

What MicroAire is being asked to decide is narrower and harder: whether ACT can be relied on to buy on a schedule and pay on terms. That is a question about how a company is run, not about how large a market is. It cannot be answered with adjectives.

So this dossier answers it the only way it can be answered, which is by opening the workings. Where a number comes from a public source, the source is named. Where it comes from ACT's own financial model, the calculation is shown.

One thing worth saying plainly Colombia scores 37 out of 100 on the 2025 Corruption Perceptions Index, ranked 99th of 182 and below the regional average of 42. The risk in this market is not demand. It is counterparty risk. A distributor that expects to be trusted here should expect to be audited, and should make that easy rather than awkward.

The comparison that gives those numbers their weight is the global one. Worldwide, procedures fell 4.8% in 2024, to 37.9 million. Colombia rose 10%. In a year when the industry contracted, this market added 43,676 procedures.

Colombia also draws patients from outside: roughly one in three is international, double the world average, behind only Tunisia and the United Arab Emirates. That matters for a capital equipment decision because it means demand is not capped by domestic purchasing power.

Seen from outside, the Colombian market looks like eight or nine competing brands. The customs record shows something simpler: three importing houses account for the equipment that competes with PAL. Everything else is a brand sitting inside one of them.

Two things follow from that table, and both are useful to MicroAire.

The first is that MicroAire is already competitive on volume. Suministros Médicos imports almost as much as Visión Médica, which carries five suppliers to their one. On a per-brand basis MicroAire is outperforming the multibrand house.

The second is that Gilmédica is not really a competitor in this category. Apyx is a small share of a very large catalogue. Their 149,235 is spread across a dozen brands, so the AYON volume behind it is modest.

Where PAL sits on price At COP 116.7 million installed, MicroAire PAL is the least expensive dedicated power-assisted system in the market: below Vibrofit at 130.0 million, below Nouvag Liposurg at 140.9 million, and far below APYX AYON at 440.0 million. The brand is not fighting a price disadvantage in Colombia. It is fighting a coverage and service gap.

On top of that sits 10% for freight, duty and nationalisation, and 19% VAT which is recoverable and therefore does not sit in the cost. That gives a landed cost of COP 56.2 million per system against a market price of COP 116.7 million.

Why the service line matters more than its size suggests A service event costs USD 3,000, of which Novatecnica takes the whole amount for parts and labour, and the surgeon pays USD 5,000. At 10 events a month across an installed base of 350 units, each machine comes in roughly once every 2.9 years. Annualised, that is 4.9% of the equipment value per year, which is exactly the level the market considers reasonable and well under what the current arrangement costs a surgeon. Nobody in the customs record offers this today.

SevenWhat the surgeon is offered, and who pays for it

ACT finances the surgeon: 50% on signature and 50% over four months. The rate is 18.99% a year, and it is not a number that was chosen for how it sounds. It is calculated to sit inside a band with two hard edges.

The usable band is 1.49 percentage points wide, and it is narrowing: in August it was 1.77. ACT charges inside it, which means the surgeon genuinely pays less than a bank would charge and ACT genuinely covers its cost of capital. None of this is funded by MicroAire.

EightThe five requests, and what each one unlocks

  1. Payment terms: 90 days written into the agreement With 120 days as a milestone tied to demonstrated payment performance. This is the only request that changes the financial model, and everything in section six depends on it.
  2. Training and education Cadaver workshops in Bogotá, access to international key opinion leaders, and the webinar programme. For Endotine alone the plan calls for four hands-on events and six webinars a year. This is what converts a registration into sales.
  3. Marketing and clinical evidence Case studies, clinical papers and presence at Colombian congresses. Local publications with Colombian surgeons carry more weight in this market than international ones.
  4. Regulatory support Documentation for the INVIMA submission, and title transfer once granted so the registration sits with ACT long term. ACT funds the process and MicroAire reimburses it, so the cost is neutral. The eight-month timeline is not.
  5. Technical and service Warranty process, complaint handling and service protocol. Without these the service line described in section five is a promise rather than a product.
