MicroAire is not being asked to believe that Colombia is a good market. The procedure counts are published by ISAPS and republished by the Colombian Society of Plastic Surgery, and anyone can check them.

What MicroAire is being asked to decide is narrower and harder: whether ACT can be relied on to buy on a schedule and pay on terms. That is a question about how a company is run, not about how large a market is. It cannot be answered with adjectives.

So this dossier answers it the only way it can be answered, which is by opening the workings. Where a number comes from a public source, the source is named. Where it comes from ACT's own financial model, the calculation is shown.

One thing worth saying plainly Colombia scores 37 out of 100 on the 2025 Corruption Perceptions Index, ranked 99th of 182 and below the regional average of 42. The risk in this market is not demand. It is counterparty risk. A distributor that expects to be trusted here should expect to be audited, and should make that easy rather than awkward.

Two things follow from that table, and both are useful to MicroAire.

The first is that MicroAire is already competitive on volume. Suministros Médicos imports almost as much as Visión Médica, which carries five suppliers to their one. On a per-brand basis MicroAire is outperforming the multibrand house.

The second is that the direct threat is Vibrofit, not AYON. Gilmédica is a large catalogue house and their 149,235 is spread across a dozen brands, so the AYON volume behind it is modest and at USD 143,217 it competes in a different price class altogether. Vibrofit is the one that sits beside PAL: power-assisted, in the same category, at USD 42,314. It is also the reason Gilmédica runs a service line, and why ACT treats service as a requirement rather than an extra.

Where PAL sits on price At USD 37,972 installed, MicroAire PAL is the least expensive dedicated power-assisted system in the market: below Vibrofit at USD 42,314, below Nouvag Liposurg at USD 45,862, and far below APYX AYON at USD 143,217. The brand is not fighting a price disadvantage in Colombia. It is fighting a coverage and service gap.

What the surgeon is offered, and who pays for it

ACT finances the surgeon: 50% on signature and 50% over four months. The rate is 18.99% a year, and it is not a number that was chosen for how it sounds. It is calculated to sit inside a band with two hard edges.

Three things happen in a fixed order, and the order is what makes the model work. MicroAire invoices on 90 days. The surgeon pays half on signature and the balance over four months. The service line collects in the same month it is delivered, with no import and no financing behind it. Money arrives before it has to leave.

And the exchange rate, since ACT buys in dollars and sells at a peso price The gross margin per system is 56.0% at today's TRM of 3,072.27. At a 10% devaluation it is 51.6%, at 20% it is 47.2%, and it only reaches zero at 6,979 pesos, which is 127% above today. Each 10% of devaluation costs about USD 40,000 a year across the 24 systems. It is a real exposure and it is a survivable one, which is worth saying plainly rather than leaving to be asked.

On top of that sits 10% for freight, duty and nationalisation, and 19% VAT which is recoverable and therefore does not sit in the cost. That gives a landed cost of USD 16,716 per system against a market price of USD 37,972, a gross margin of 56.0%. It was 51.8% in July: the peso strengthened from 3,362 to 3,072 and the margin moved on its own, without anyone touching a price.

Why the service line matters more than its size suggests A service event costs USD 3,000, of which Novatecnica takes the whole amount for parts and labour, and the surgeon pays USD 5,000. At 10 events a month across an installed base of 350 units, each machine comes in roughly once every 2.9 years. Annualised, that is 4.9% of the equipment value per year, which is exactly the level the market considers reasonable and well under what the current arrangement costs a surgeon. Visión Médica and Gilmédica both run a service line for their own brands. It is the norm in this market, and MicroAire is the brand in Colombia without one.

Slide 8How the consumables actually move

The consumables line is the part worth reading twice, because a console is not a sale. It is the first of several years of them, and MicroAire's own order book shows the pattern without any help from us: in 2025 the channel placed 22 consoles and pulled 87 cables and 31 handpieces along behind them in the same year. Roughly four cables for every console placed.

Four mechanisms move that volume. Bulk ordering already works and continues. The other three are choices, and the first of them is a deliberate decision not to do the obvious thing.

Why ACT will not discount, and what it does instead The peso moved. The equipment costs less to land than it did while the surgeon pays what he always paid, and from where he sits that reads as the brand keeping the currency gain. The obvious answer is to cut the price. It is also the wrong one: it is hard to undo, it trains the market to wait for the next drop, and it hands margin away permanently in exchange for a swing that will reverse.

So ACT packages instead. The price stays where it is and what the surgeon receives grows. A cannula lasts about twenty procedures, and in practice surgeons run them well past that, because cannulas are expensive rather than because replacements are slow. So the package carries a set of cannulas he chooses himself, together with the items that are bought rarely and therefore never rotate.

The cannula that travels with every service

The same idea rides on the service line, and this one is arithmetic rather than intention. A service is priced at USD 5,000 today and costs USD 3,000, which Novatécnica takes for parts and labour. The change: the price goes to USD 5,200 and every machine that comes in for maintenance leaves with a cannula included.

Slide 9What ACT asks from MicroAire

  1. Payment terms: 90 days written into the agreement With 120 days as a milestone tied to demonstrated payment performance. This is the only request that changes the financial model, and everything in section six depends on it.
  2. Training and education Cadaver workshops in Bogotá, access to international key opinion leaders, and the webinar programme. For Endotine alone the plan calls for four hands-on events and six webinars a year, and the workshop is now costed: USD 20,722 for ten surgeons. ACT charges the surgeon USD 2,200 to attend, which covers it and little else, and gives the first two away entirely. ACT asks MicroAire to fund half of each workshop. This is what converts a registration into sales.
  3. Marketing and clinical evidence Case studies, clinical papers and presence at Colombian congresses. Local publications with Colombian surgeons carry more weight in this market than international ones.

    To be explicit about who pays for what, since this is the request that is easiest to leave vague: ACT designs, builds and staffs the congress stand and carries the marketing budget, USD 39,000 a year. What ACT asks MicroAire for is speakers and course slots, roughly USD 20,000 a year — faculty time and places at meetings ACT is already attending, not a cash transfer.
  4. Regulatory support Documentation for the INVIMA submission. To be plain about what is being asked here: MicroAire owns Endotine and owns its registrations. What ACT asks is to be named as the holder of the Colombian sanitary registration while it distributes the product, because that is the entity INVIMA deals with for imports and vigilance. The registration follows the representation. If the representation ever moves, so does it. ACT funds the process and MicroAire reimburses it, so the cost is neutral. The eight-month timeline is not.
  5. Technical and service Warranty process, complaint handling and service protocol. Without these the service line described in section five is a promise rather than a product.

Slide 10Why the Colombian representation should sit with ACT

What the channel generates, and what comes back The channel bought USD 522,500 from MicroAire in 2025. Landed at the same 10% import surcharge ACT pays, that is USD 574,750 of cost. Sold at the margin ACT documents on its own systems, 56.0%, it implies roughly USD 731,500 of gross margin a year. Two independent routes agree on the order of magnitude: the Estudio Financiero put the channel's public sales at COP 3,200 to 3,800 million (USD 1.04 to 1.24 million), and this calculation lands at COP 4,014 million, or USD 1.31 million.

Set against that pool: one cadaver workshop costs USD 20,722 all in, which is 2.8% of it, and has never been run. ACT's whole marketing, congress, training and workshop budget, USD 90,259 a year, is 12.3% of it. The money to build a service line, train surgeons and staff a congress stand has been there the entire time.

None of this is a complaint, and the USD 731,500 is an estimate built from ACT's own margin rather than from anybody's books, which is why the arithmetic is shown rather than the conclusion. The middle column took two hours to establish from customs records and public sources, which is the real point: the work was available to anyone who wanted it.
